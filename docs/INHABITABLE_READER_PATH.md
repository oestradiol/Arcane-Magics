# Inhabitable Reader Path

**Purpose:** keep Venus-Minerva human-readable when the formal machinery becomes dense.

This is not a new theory layer. It is a recurring reader interface for asking what an abstraction means for an indexed center that must actually receive consequences, orient, act, and remain corrigible.

## 1. Return to the lived cycle

When a section becomes difficult, reduce it to five questions:

1. **Receive** — what consequence, signal, constraint, or difference enters?
2. **Locate** — where does it land, and for which indexed center?
3. **Move** — what continuation becomes admissible, selected, or enacted?
4. **Return** — what can come back that the center did not pre-author?
5. **Redistribute** — what changes locally while enough continuity survives to keep learning?

```text
receive
-> locate
-> move
-> return
-> redistribute
-> receive
```

This is a didactic projection of the larger Meta-Dynamics recurrence, not a replacement for its typed formal objects.

## 2. Three incidence questions

A useful human-scale decomposition is:

```text
world-incidence
carrier exchange
self-incidence
```

Ask:

- **world-incidence:** what in the surrounding field is actually reaching the center?
- **carrier exchange:** what must be taken in, spent, transmitted, repaired, or maintained for the center to continue?
- **self-incidence:** what tells the center where it is, how it is oriented, and whether it remains viable?

These need not be literal sensory or biological channels in every register. They are prompts for preventing an abstract model from forgetting presentation, maintenance cost, and local condition.

## 3. Relation without possession

A healthy relational model should preserve three terms:

```text
self-for-self
other-for-other
relation-without-possession
```

That means:

- a center may preserve itself;
- another center may remain independently indexed;
- relation does not require ownership, fusion, or forced agreement.

> unity without appropriation; difference without exile; relation without self-erasure; meaning without closure.

This is not a complete ethical theory.

## 4. Care as maintenance of a relational field

Maintenance, defense, refusal, and selective destruction are not automatically opposed to care.

> Does an intervention preserve the conditions under which legitimate difference, future correction, and continued relation remain possible, or does it preserve one local organization by erasing every inconvenient return?

Care therefore includes maintenance of correction channels, not indiscriminate permeability.

## 5. Memory as altered future admissibility

When the word *memory* becomes too object-like, ask what has changed in the future:

```text
history
-> retained change
-> altered future admissibility
```

A system remembers when what happened changes what it can subsequently notice, predict, select, resist, reconstruct, or become.

This is broader than stored records and narrower than claiming every changed state is meaningful memory.

## 6. Stability without stasis

A humane learning system should support both:

```text
new experience can alter the model
AND
not every new experience destroys the model
```

and:

```text
another center can return something unpredicted
AND
its difference is not automatically treated as error
```

The target is neither closure nor dissolution. It is maintained structure that remains reopenable.

## 7. Selective permeability

Living and learning systems require boundaries.

The useful question is not whether a boundary exists, but whether it remains selectively permeable to:

- correction;
- nourishment or resource exchange;
- disagreement;
- independent evidence;
- repair;
- exit;
- new relation.

A boundary that admits everything cannot maintain a center. A boundary that admits nothing cannot learn.

## 8. Human cost is part of the system

Any research architecture that depends on destroying the carriers who maintain it has failed an operational conservation test.

When evaluating a workflow, ask:

- what does participation cost?
- who absorbs the cost?
- is maintenance visible?
- can people refuse, pause, fork, or exit?
- does the system require chronic emergency to reproduce itself?

Human sustainability is not evidence that a claim is true. It is a condition for keeping correction channels alive.

## 9. How to use this throughout the repository

Reader-facing material should periodically translate abstract machinery into at least one of:

- an incidence question;
- a receive/locate/move/return/redistribute cycle;
- a maintenance-cost question;
- a preserved-difference question;
- a concrete reopening route.

Do not add these mechanically to every paragraph. Their job is to restore inhabitation where abstraction has outrun the reader.

## 10. Minimal humane invariant

> Preserve enough selfhood for a center to continue, enough otherness for genuine return to exist, enough relation for consequences to cross the boundary, and enough openness for what returns to matter.


## 11. Local closure, WITHHOLD, and Residual

A useful plain-language picture is:

> A center may be complete enough to continue without pretending to contain everything that can still matter to it.

That is a **returnable local closure**.

Its boundary should preserve enough identity for the center to remain a center, while still allowing independently sourced consequence to matter:

```text
relation without fusion
+
boundary without isolation
```

When two possibilities still lead to distinguishable futures, do not flatten them merely because the current model would be simpler without the distinction.

```text
still future-separating
-> Residual
-> WITHHOLD collapse
-> wait for / seek the separator
```

When no admissible future can distinguish them at the declared scope, the difference may be consumable as gauge.

This reader-level picture is carrier-neutral. The physics-facing PPD-Crystal / horizon-rebarb program is one possible realization, not the definition.
