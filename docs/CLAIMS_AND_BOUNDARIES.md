# Claims and boundaries

The same sentence can report an experience, propose an ontology, offer a mathematical model, and suggest a mechanism. Those are different claims even when one piece of prose contains all four. Venus × Minerva keeps their warrant separate so a meaningful native object is neither inflated into a stronger claim nor erased because the stronger claim failed.

## Claim and register families

- `REL/STRUCT`: structural relation;
- `EPI`: epistemic or warrant claim;
- `ONT`: explicit model-ontological articulation;
- `FORM`: formal or mathematical claim;
- `EMP`: empirical or scientific claim;
- `PHEN`: phenomenological occurrence or structure;
- `THEO`: theological or ultimate-orientation interpretation;
- `NORM`: normative or governance claim;
- `HIST`: historical or provenance claim;
- `ENG`: implementation or engineering claim.

A statement may occupy more than one register. Warrant does not automatically transfer among them.

For example, a lived occurrence can be licensed as `PHEN` while its proposed external source remains `UNDETERMINED`; a formal correspondence can be valid while a mechanism identity is unsupported; a historical resemblance can deserve `HISTORICAL-PRIORITY` without becoming project origin.

## Common forbidden promotions

```text
phenomenal occurrence -/-> external source attribution
symbolic fit -/-> mechanism identity
formal consistency -/-> empirical truth
successful model -/-> ontological identity
historical recurrence -/-> direct inheritance
centrality -/-> truth sovereignty
implementation success -/-> authorization
```

## Native-object non-annihilation

Failure to establish a stronger bridge does not erase an object independently licensed at a native register. Conversely, native legitimacy does not repair the failed stronger bridge.
