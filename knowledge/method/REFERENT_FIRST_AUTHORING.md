# Referent-first authoring

A technical name can help a reader compress a distinction. It can also decide the distinction for the reader before the reader has seen the thing being named. Venus × Minerva therefore treats naming as an intervention.

The writing order is:

```text
referent
→ relation
→ scope / index
→ sign
→ term
```

The first occurrence of a loaded term must point to the thing or relation it abbreviates. A later definition cannot fully undo a misleading first association.

## Three rules

1. **Do not be redundant in the world.** A local term is earned only when existing language fails to preserve a consequential distinction at the declared task or index.
2. **Name the referent before the sign.** Describe what happens, what differs, and what later consequence changes before introducing project vocabulary.
3. **Treat terminology as causal.** Signs are manipulated by readers, institutions, models, and tools. A sign can reorganize available associations and therefore alter inference or action.

A fourth distinction follows:

```text
information != informing another center
```

A difference may become consequential without a communicator. A communicator may present a difference without changing what the receiver later does. Informing another center does not author that center's uptake.

See [Information and informing](../problems/INFORMATION_AND_INFORMING.md).

## Deletion test

After a term is introduced, remove it.

A section passes when a competent reader can still:

- restate the underlying distinction in ordinary or domain-native language;
- identify the scope and the nearest counterexample;
- compare the object with prior art;
- explain what evidence would make the local term unnecessary.

If the distinction disappears with the sign, the sign was carrying more authority than the referent.

## Cross-reference rule

A node should not repeat a neighboring node merely to appear self-contained. State the local distinction, link to the dependency, and record what changes at this node.

The target is not a reader who remembers our vocabulary. The target is a reader who can reconstruct, criticize, rename, transfer, and discard it.
