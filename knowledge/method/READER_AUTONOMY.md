# Reader autonomy

Transmission succeeds when another person gains a usable distinction without becoming dependent on the source vocabulary or founder.

A paper can be memorable and still fail this test. Exact repetition may preserve representation while destroying reconstruction.

## Intended trajectory

```text
encounter
→ discriminate
→ reconstruct
→ compare
→ attack
→ transfer
→ author
```

A reader should become better able to:

- restate the relation without project names;
- locate the nearest established alternative;
- identify which evidence supports which register;
- generate counterexamples;
- replace a broken mechanism while retaining unaffected results;
- request less founder interpretation over time.

## What autonomy is not

Reader autonomy does not mean independence from evidence, primary sources, proof, participant authority, domain expertise, or returned consequence.

The desired reduction is:

```text
founder/private-thread dependence ↓
while
source/evidence/reality dependence remains appropriately live
```

## Prospective test

A future source-removal study should compare paper-only, repository-supported, structured-ledger, and neutral-placeholder conditions. Useful outcomes include vocabulary-free reconstruction, dependency accuracy, register accuracy, counterexample quality, terminology replacement, novel transfer, and requests for founder rescue.

Until such a study exists, reader autonomy is a design criterion, not an empirical achievement.
