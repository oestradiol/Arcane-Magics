# A model of another center is not the other center

An internal critic can surprise its generator while still being generated inside the same controlled system. Another person, institution, environment, or independent process can instead return a consequence whose claim-relevant variation was not fixed by the focal model in advance.

The distinction is:

```text
feedback != independent correction
surprise != truth
Other != model(Other)
```

"Non-preauthored" is claim-relative. An action may help cause what returns. The relevant question is whether the model whose claim is being tested already fixed the variation that later appears to correct it.

This prevents two opposite mistakes:

1. treating every surprising output as independent evidence;
2. demanding total causal independence from a world the agent already acted within.

## Alterity and participant jurisdiction

Levinas is a historical-priority neighbor for the irreducibility of the Other to a totalizing representation. Participatory sense-making adds a different result: interaction can acquire higher-order dynamics while differentiated participants retain autonomy. Longino shows why criticism from differently situated agents matters for objectivity.

The project residual candidate is operational rather than metaphysical: track source, exposure, authorship, authorization, affected-party incidence, and returned consequence so that another center cannot be silently replaced by its model.

## Reconstruction check

Design a test where an internal simulator and an independently authoring source produce equally surprising outputs. What additional provenance would let you distinguish surprise from correction?
