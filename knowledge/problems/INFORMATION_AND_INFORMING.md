# A difference that matters is not the same event as telling someone

A temperature change can trigger a thermostat without anyone communicating. A warning can be delivered to a person and still fail to alter what they infer or do. These are different relations.

At a declared system boundary, call a difference **informational** when its incidence can originate or change a later admissible action or transformation.

Separately, one center may act so as to present a difference to another center. That is an act of **informing**.

```text
Information(Δ, S)
= Δ can originate or change a later transformation of S

Inform(i, j, Δ)
= center i acts so that Δ is presented to center j
```

Therefore:

```text
information != message
information != informing-someone
delivery != uptake != retained consequence
informing(j) != authorship-over-j
```

A receiver can reject, reinterpret, forget, transform, or act on a message in an unanticipated way. The receiver's resulting interpretation remains locally authored.

This distinction matters for AI systems, education, politics, therapy, scientific communication, and any theory of semantic transport. Treating delivery as uptake makes the sender appear causally sovereign over the receiver.

## Nearest prior art

Bateson's cybernetic formulation of information as a difference that makes a difference is a historical-priority neighbor. Shannon information answers a different mathematical question. Speech-act, communication, and enactive traditions address additional parts of the sender/receiver relation.

The project residual is narrower: combine consequential difference with indexed authorship, provenance/exposure, jurisdiction, and later-admissibility change without collapsing information into communication.
