# Seeing a projection does not mean you can reconstruct its source

A shadow can preserve some structure while deleting depth. A statistical summary can preserve a task-relevant quantity while erasing the trajectory that produced it. A current self-presentation can remain stable across different choices of observable.

Formally, if a source is mapped into a presentation, several non-entailments matter:

```text
projection exists -/-> section exists
section exists -/-> unique section
section exists -/-> cheap section
same projection -/-> same source trajectory
observable consequence -/-> regenerative source access
```

A right inverse, when it exists, is an additional object with its own cost, scope, and warrant.

This distinction became especially important in project archaeology: historical fixed-point "Self" implementations were stable only relative to chosen observable projections. Projection-relative stable presentation was therefore retyped away from observer-independent Self substance.

## Neighboring formalisms

Quotients, sections, lenses, bidirectional transformations, causal abstraction, system identification, and inverse problems provide mature neighboring machinery. Project-specific terms must earn residual value against those alternatives.

## Reader check

Find a representation you use every day. List at least two different source histories that could produce the same representation. What extra information would be required to reconstruct which history occurred?
