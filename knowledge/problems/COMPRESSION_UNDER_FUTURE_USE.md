# A good compression now can become a bad compression after you act through it

Two histories may be equivalent for a current prediction task. If a policy acts on the compressed representation, a distinction erased as irrelevant now may become decisive later.

Static sufficiency therefore does not automatically imply sufficiency under recursive use.

Let `q` compress state for task `T`. The project calls the stronger condition **recursive sufficiency** only when the compression remains adequate under the future transformations induced by acting, updating, or governing through it.

This belongs beside, not above, established work on state abstraction, bisimulation, causal abstraction, computational mechanics, coarse-graining, and sufficient statistics.

## Why provenance can re-enter

Two current states can look identical while carrying different source or exposure histories. If those histories later change trust, authorization, update policy, or reachable states, provenance is operative state rather than decorative metadata.

## Reduction test

If an established abstraction formalism captures the same future-use condition at the declared task without losing any decision-relevant distinction, retire the local name.
